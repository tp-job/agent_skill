# Engagement activities — get the class doing, not just watching

Part of **Draw**. Slides alone encourage passive watching, and Northern Illinois University's guide lists that as PowerPoint's main risk: reduced interaction, information overload, and slides that "drive" the lesson. Build participation into the deck on purpose.

Source keys: **[NIU]** Northern Illinois University · **[COM]** COMSIAM · **[TT]** Teachers Tech (full list in [sources](sources.md)).

**Provenance:** the activity names and prompts are from NIU's *Teaching with PowerPoint*, Part III (each can be one slide). The quiz and game types come from COMSIAM, and the live poll from Teachers Tech. The "use at" column is this skill's own assessment.

---

## 1. Activities from NIU

| Activity | Prompt on the slide | Use at | Time (assessment) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Opening question** | "Take a moment to reflect on ___." | as students arrive | 2–3 min |
| **Think-Pair-Share** | think → turn to a partner → share with the class | after a chunk | 3–5 min |
| **Focused listing** | "List as many characteristics of ___ as you can" | opening or recall | 2 min |
| **Brainstorming** | "What do you know about ___? Start with your clearest thoughts, then the more 'out there' ones" | topic start | 3–5 min |
| **Questions checkpoint** | "Any questions?" about every 15 minutes | between chunks | 1–2 min |
| **Note check** | "Compare notes with a partner", or "summarise the most important information", or "clarify sticking points" | after a chunk | 2–3 min |
| **Question-and-answer pairs** | "Think of one question, then try to stump your partner" | after a chunk | 3 min |
| **Two-minute paper** | "Summarise the most important points of today" | end | 2 min |
| **One last question** | "If you could ask one last question, what would it be?" | end | 2 min |
| **Classroom opinion poll** | "Do you believe in ___?" | to open discussion | 2–3 min |
| **Muddiest point** | "What parts still confuse you?" (anonymous) | end, feeds the next lesson | 2 min |
| **Most useful point** | "What was most useful today, and how would you use it?" | end | 2 min |

**Quick pick:** to check understanding, use **Muddiest Point** or **Two-minute paper**. To warm up a room, use an **Opening question**. To slow down and consolidate, use **Think-Pair-Share**.

## 2. Interactive slides and quizzes

| Idea | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quiz** | multiple-choice slides after a key section, with clickable buttons for answers and instant feedback | [TT] [COM] |
| **Games** | a question game, matching, fill in the blank | [COM] |
| **Live poll** | an add-in such as Poll Everywhere puts a poll inside the slide | [TT] |
| **Write-on slides** | type into the slide during the show to project students' ideas, then save them into the file. It needs set-up beforehand | [NIU] |
| **Pen and highlighter** | during the show, use the pen, highlighter or laser tool | [NIU] |
| **Hyperlinks** | link to a resource or another slide so learners can explore | [TT] |
| **Blank screen** | press **B** (black) or **W** (white) to pause and get attention; press again to resume | [NIU] |

**Multimodal delivery:** NIU says presenting through several modes (visual, audio, reading and writing, and interactive) improves comprehension and recall. It gives no citation for that claim, so treat it as reasonable practice, not a finding.

## 3. Rules

- **One activity per block, with time reserved.** An activity squeezed to 30 seconds is a rhetorical question.
- **Prepare the prompt.** Put the exact wording on a slide. Improvised prompts are vague.
- **Debrief every activity.** Ask what happened and what it shows, tied to the target.
- **Match the activity to the target.** A poll won't teach a procedure. See [teaching-methods](teaching-methods.md).
- **Keep the lights on.** A dark room hurts note-taking, and NIU says it invites dozing.

---

## When not to use

- **A five-minute explanation** doesn't need three activities. One check question is enough.
- **A learner group that isn't ready to share** (new group, sensitive topic). Use anonymous formats such as Muddiest Point or a written paper, not public polls.
- **Tools you haven't tested.** A live poll that fails in front of the room costs more than it gains. Test it, and have a paper fallback.
