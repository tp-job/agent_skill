**Sources, read in full:**

| Key | Source | Kind |

| **[R]** | Iveta Pavlova, *Web Design Trends 2026: The Next Level of Digital Creativity*, reallygooddesigns.com. Published about late 2025, a forecast written before the year began. | 10 trends, a curated gallery drawn mostly from Awwwards winners |

**What the sources do and don't contain:** neither cites statistics. [R] gives no caveats, accessibility notes or performance notes. Every **Risk**, **Guard**, **Build** and **Shelf life** line below is *this skill's assessment*, not the authors'. [R] draws its examples from award sites, which over-represent agencies and portfolios. Discount accordingly for products, commerce and public services.

## Baseline — apply all five

From [C]. Each is written as a rule, with the author's own test, then how to check it.

| # | Rule [C] | Author's test | How to check (assessment) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | **Personality.** Stand out instead of fitting in. [C] names *tactile maximalism* (rich texture, vibrant colour, magazine-spread layouts) as the reaction to sterile minimalism. | Could this site belong to any business in its industry? If yes, it has no personality. | Swap the logo for a competitor's. If nothing else looks wrong, the site fails. Personality can be quiet: a strong voice and one distinctive choice count. Maximalism is one route, not the rule. |
| B2 | **Motion with intention.** Micro-interactions that guide, confirm and clarify, not theatre. | Does each animation guide attention, confirm an action, or improve clarity? If not, remove it. | Every animation has a stated job, respects `prefers-reduced-motion`, and anything that auto-moves for more than 5 s can be paused (WCAG 2.2.2). |
| B3 | **AI as copilot, not replacement.** Automate palettes, font pairings, code and accessibility flags. Keep brand judgement human. | Which tasks are repetitive and low-judgement? Automate those first. | AI output is a draft that a human signs off. Generated imagery never stands in for real people, customers or testimonials. |
| B4 | **Accessibility is non-negotiable,** built in from day one, not a final QA step. [C] notes a growing legal and reputational case. | Run WAVE or Axe during design and development, not after. | WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text and UI, visible focus, a real heading structure, targets ≥ 24 px. Automated tools catch only part of it, so also tab through the page by hand. |
| B5 | **Fluid.** Works on every screen from watch to widescreen. The next step is personalisation by context: first-time vs returning visitor, location, time, traffic source. | Audit across devices, then try one small personalisation, such as a headline set by traffic source. | Test at 375 / 768 / 1440 px and 200% zoom. Personalisation needs consent under GDPR or Thailand's PDPA, and a sensible default for visitors who decline it. |

**B1 and the statements below overlap.** Personality is the *why*. A statement trend is one possible *how*.

## Statements — choose one, at most two

The trend definitions come from [R]. The scoring columns are the assessment, using the scale in the skill's §3 (3 = best).

| # | Trend [R] | Visible traits | Cost | A11y risk | Shelf life |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | **AI-enhanced creativity:** AI helps generate layouts, type and visuals, and sites adapt content per user in real time | a sleek "futuristic" look, generated imagery, personalised blocks | 2 | 2 | 1 (the generated-image look dates fast) |
| S2 | **Virtual-reality websites:** walk-through 3D worlds and digital showrooms instead of flat pages | first-person navigation, spatial scenes | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| S3 | **Dynamic motion design:** motion as brand identity, like logos that unfold and scroll animation in the brand's rhythm | an animated logo that becomes the nav, a consistent motion language | 2 | 2 | 3 (a motion *system* ages like a type system) |
| S4 | **Interactive 3D models:** objects that spin, tilt or react to cursor and scroll | product previews, cursor-reactive shapes | 1–2 | 2 | 2 |
| S5 | **Exploratory layouts:** scattered, floating or modular elements that reveal content on click or hover, like a canvas or map | a broken grid, click-to-reveal | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| S6 | **Typographic statements:** oversized, mostly sans-serif type carries the whole design; type that stretches and unravels on scroll | giant type, flat or black-and-white backdrops | 3 | 2 | 3 |
| S7 | **Cinematic heroes:** full-bleed photo or video with oversized plain type on top | a full-screen hero, minimal copy | 2 | 2 | 3 (a long-running pattern that [R] says "continues") |
| S8 | **Mixed scroll directions:** vertical scrolling that turns sideways for galleries and timelines | horizontal chapters | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| S9 | **Noise & chromatic mash-ups:** neon gradients over grainy backgrounds, glitch and negative effects on images and type | glow, grain, chromatic offset | 3 | 1 | 1 (strongly year-stamped) |
| S10 | **Illustrated worlds:** doodles, cartoons and hand-drawn systems, even several styles mixed on one page | hand-drawn line, playful micro-interactions | 1 (commissioned art) | 3 | 2 ([R] calls illustration long-lasting; a mixed-style *collage* dates faster) |

Thesis fit isn't in the table because it depends on the brand. Score it per project.

### Fits best / guard / build

| # | Fits best | Guard (required if adopted) | Build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | products that genuinely personalise | disclose what is personalised; honour consent; keep a non-personalised default | server-side variants or edge middleware; no layout shift when content swaps |
| S2 | showrooms, events, games, real estate | a flat page with the same content and actions; never the only path to buy or contact | three.js / R3F; budget the payload; poster image first |
| S3 | brands with a strong mark and rhythm | `prefers-reduced-motion` gives a static logo; no motion blocks input | CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: view()`) as progressive enhancement, or GSAP ScrollTrigger; View Transitions for page-to-page |
| S4 | physical products people want to handle | a static image fallback; keyboard controls for rotation; lazy-load below the fold | `<model-viewer>` for a simple product spin; R3F for custom scenes; compressed glTF (Draco/Meshopt) |
| S5 | portfolios, studios, archives | a plain list view of the same content; clear hover *and* focus states; content present in the DOM, not fetched on click, so it is indexed | CSS grid with deliberate overlaps; `<details>` or buttons with `aria-expanded` for reveals |
| S6 | writing, editorial, agencies, launches | real text, not text in images; readable at 200% zoom; animated words settle to a static state | fluid type with `clamp()`; variable fonts; subset and `font-display: swap` |
| S7 | hospitality, travel, film, premium products | a pause control on video (WCAG 2.2.2); text contrast measured over the *brightest* frame; a poster image | `<video muted autoplay loop playsinline poster>`; a scrim gradient under the type; a smaller encode for mobile |
| S8 | storytelling, timelines, galleries | never hijack the wheel; keyboard and touch must work; a visible cue for the direction change | `overflow-x` with `scroll-snap`, or a pinned section with ScrollTrigger; test with a trackpad and a mouse wheel |
| S9 | music, festivals, youth brands, experimental studios | no flashing above 3 per second (WCAG 2.3.1); keep grain and chromatic effects off body text | SVG `feTurbulence` or a small tiled noise PNG for grain; `mix-blend-mode`; text-shadow offsets for chromatic splits, on display type only |
| S10 | consumer brands, education, onboarding, empty states | alt text for meaningful illustrations; SVG optimised; one style guide for the illustrators | inline SVG for animated parts; Lottie or Rive for character motion |

## Year notes

- **Continuing from earlier years:** cinematic heroes ([R] says explicitly that they continue), typographic statements, illustration and 3D. These are established patterns now. They aren't news, and they age slowly.
- **The two sources pull in opposite directions:** [C] asks for *intention* and restraint in motion, while [R] celebrates motion and effects as identity. Settle it per project: [C]'s motion test (B2) is a baseline, so any motion taken from [R] has to pass it.
- **The most likely clichés by 2027** (assessment): glitch and chromatic mash-ups, generated "futuristic" imagery, and walk-through 3D worlds used where a page would do.
